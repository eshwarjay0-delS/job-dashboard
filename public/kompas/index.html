Common mistakes

What goes wrong most often when people answer aloud, how to hear it in yourself, and what to do instead. The figures are measured on 1,103 real interview answers (122,952 words, 65 recorded sessions).

The mistakes, most common firsthow often it happens
1. Filler words

Sounds like: "uh", "um", "you know", "basically", "so yeah".

Do this: say nothing. A short pause sounds like thinking; "uh" sounds like searching.

"Uh" alone is 81% of all fillers. Only 3% of longer answers are nearly free of them.

8.6in every 100 words, one every 12 words
2. Opening on a filler

Sounds like: "Yeah, like…", "Uh, I…", "Yeah, so…", "So, uh…".

Do this: take one silent second, then start on the first real word.

The start is the worst part of an answer: fillers run at 10 per 100 in the first 12 words and 8.4 after. The first word is "yeah" 18% of the time, "uh" 17%, "so" 9%. "Yeah, I led that…" is fine; "Yeah, so, like, uh…" is the problem.

50%of answers open this way
3. Chaining thoughts with "so" and "and"

Sounds like: one sentence that never ends: "…and then we, so basically it, and after that…".

Do this: put a full stop where you would say "so" or "and". It also gives you the pause that replaces "uh".

20% of sentences start with "so" and 10% with "and".

14%of sentences run past 25 words
4. False starts

Sounds like: "the, the", "we are, are", or a sentence dropped after one word ("The. So we…").

Do this: finish the thought in your head, then say it. Slower is clearer.

1repeated word in every 100
5. "Like" as padding

Sounds like: "we had, like, fifty locations", "it was like a migration".

Do this: drop it. "We had fifty locations."

4.1times in every 100 words
6. Too short, or far too long

Sounds like: a one-line reply to a real question, or an answer that is still going after two minutes.

Do this: for a real question, aim for one to two minutes: about 150 to 250 words.

Half of all answers are under 55 words, and 31% are under 30.

12%of answers run past two minutes
7. Trailing off, with no ending

Sounds like: "…so yeah.", "…and, uh, yeah.", "…you know.", "…like that."

Do this: land the last line. Say what the work led to, good or bad: what improved, what stayed stuck, what it cost. Then stop.

23% of answers end on a trailing phrase.

38%of answers about work you did never say what it led to
8. No concrete number

Sounds like: "a lot of servers", "many users", "for a while".

Do this: if you have a true number, say it: a count, a time, a size. Never make one up.

22%of longer answers contain a number
9. Announcing the parts of a story

Sounds like: "Situation: … Task: … Action: … Result: …".

Do this: tell it as one story. Put them in the moment ("We were in the middle of…"), say what fell to you, what you did first, and how it ended.

0labels belong in a spoken answer
Three habits that remove most of itpractise these first
  1. A silent second before the first word. Half of all answers open on a filler, and the opening is where fillers are thickest.
  2. A full stop where you would say "so" or "and". It shortens the long sentences and gives you the pause that replaces "uh".
  3. Land the last line, then stop. Say what the work led to, and end there. No "so yeah".
How it looks when it goes rightfor comparison
Under 2fillers per 100 words is a clean answer
0.3hedges per 100 words ("I think", "maybe") is already low: most people state things plainly
4.5 to 1"I" against "we": say what you did yourself
9.4 → 7.9fillers per 100 words from June to September: it does improve with practice

These figures come from recordings, which can miss a word and decide where a sentence breaks. Treat them as a guide to what is common, not as exact counts.
